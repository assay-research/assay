Leaderboard Reality Check

Paste a Hyperliquid address. This reads its own fills off the public API and runs the checks a return ranking leaves out — whether it still trades, whether its equity is even visible, how many losses its edge is estimated from, and how much of its order flow your account could actually place.

Everything runs in your browser. No account, no keys, nothing sent anywhere except Hyperliquid's own public endpoint.

What a return ranking hides

These are the defects found while measuring 2,917 screened Hyperliquid accounts.

Each is a check above. The full measurement — all 47,190 ranked accounts, every exclusion counted, and what account size does to the candidate pool — is written up in What the Leaderboards Hide.

  • Dead accounts stay listed. 274 of the accounts excluded from that sweep had not traded the coin in over 45 days — 42% of all exclusions. Several were advertised at triple-digit "month ROI" after 100+ days of no trading. Staleness ran 5% across all screened accounts but 10% in the top 40 by ROI: it concentrates exactly where the ranking points.
  • Equity is read from the wrong place. Perp balance alone reports zero for anyone parking funds in spot, which silently dropped 13 of 20 qualifying accounts. The example address is a live case: its perp account value reads 0.0.
  • A perfect record is the most suspicious one. 125 accounts had no losses at all, which makes a break-even rate incomputable. The cleanest record found — 606 closed trades, not one loss — holds only one leg of a hedge; copying it takes on the exact risk the hedge exists to cancel.
  • An edge measured from one loss is noise. Ranking by margin instead of return looks rigorous until you count the losses: the top 12 had 1, 1, 1, 1, 5, 2, 1, 2, 2, 7, 3 and 1. Margin needs a minimum loss count, not just a minimum trade count.
  • Resting orders cannot be copied. An account filling most of its volume as a maker is getting prices you will never get by following it.
  • Liquidated accounts are not on the leaderboard at all, so every pool is survivorship-biased before you start — and userFills returns the most recent 2,000 fills, so what you see is a window, not a career.
This page reports measurements of public on-chain data. It does not rank accounts, recommend any account, or suggest a position size, and nothing here is investment advice. Figures come from Hyperliquid's public info endpoint at the moment you press the button; closedPnl is realised profit only, and equity is today's value applied across the whole fill window.
